The premise

Technology should earn its place.

In young children's classrooms, technology sits alongside talk, play, practical exploration, drawing, making and human interaction. The interesting question is not simply “Can we use technology?” but whether it changes what children can access, express, investigate or create.

As you explore the site, keep one question in mind:

Could the learning happen just as effectively without the technology?

If yes, why are we using it? If no, what new possibility has it created?

Not an app list.
A way of thinking.

Tap, Talk, Think is a lens for discussing purposeful technology use rather than a rigid teaching sequence. Learning may move backwards, forwards and between each idea.

Purpose before product

Starting with an app can easily lead to designing learning around what the tool can do. Starting with the learning intention changes the conversation: first identify what children need to experience, practise or understand, then decide whether technology can strengthen it.

Try asking:
• What are the children thinking about?
• What are they talking about?
• What are they creating or changing?
• What becomes possible because of the technology?

Technology in practice

The examples in this session are organised around learning, not around apps. Each one asks how technology can add something purposeful to an already strong EY or KS1 experience.

🪸

Explore what we cannot bring in

Interactive 3D and AR can give children access to coral reefs, complex objects and environments that cannot realistically sit on a classroom table. The technology becomes the stimulus; observation, questioning and talk remain the learning.

🎵

Create rather than consume

With tools such as GarageBand, young children can compose, sequence, listen back, revise and justify choices. The value is not learning where the buttons are; it is having an accessible medium for making something of their own.

🎙️

Make thinking audible

Voice recording gives young learners another way to capture an explanation. Children can rehearse, listen back, improve and demonstrate understanding without every outcome depending on writing stamina.

🔬

Amplify practical science

Photography, video, audio and digital models can sit alongside first-hand investigation. Children can capture change, revisit evidence and communicate what they think happened and why.

🧱

Know when not to use it

Digital does not automatically mean better. If handling real materials, building physically, drawing or talking gives children the richer experience, the iPad can stay closed.

🗣️

Put oracy around the screen

A device can easily become a solitary experience. Deliberate partner talk, prediction, explanation and reflection turn it into a shared object around which language and reasoning can develop.

A useful planning shift

Instead of writing “Children will use JigSpace”, begin with the learning: “Children will observe features closely, discuss what they notice and use evidence to explain their ideas.” The technology then has to justify how it supports that goal.

What I’m looking at

How can technology enhance learning for our youngest pupils without becoming the learning itself? We’ll look at access, talk, creativity, collaboration and thinking through real classroom examples — from immersive experiences and digital creation to moments when putting the device down is the better pedagogical choice.

The question running through everything I share:

What did the technology add?

Not “Was technology used?” but what became more accessible, expressive, collaborative or possible because it was there?
